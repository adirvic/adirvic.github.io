About

From a small farm to global payment systems

I grew up on a small farm in the countryside of Paraná, Brazil, and didn't touch a computer until I was 15. Owning one, or paying for a course, was out of reach. At 17 I bought my first PC and started repairing other people's machines, the one tech job open to someone with no experience.

During military service I ran IT for the battalion's first section, working every day alongside the captain and senior officers. Then came technical support, and at 22 I started college. In my first year I stood out in programming and logic, and my Java professor invited me to interview at the company where he worked. That is how I became a developer, at Softpharma, acquired by Linx two years later.

Since then I've had ups and downs, but I've always been the person teams turn to for the hardest problem of the moment. Along the way people gave me a few nicknames:

  • the JTable guy
  • the JavaFX guy
  • the WSDL guy
  • the invoice guy

At CISS I found a new SEFAZ tax-authority service that nobody had built on yet, and designed and built a complete invoice-import product around it, from idea to architecture to code. At Elo I'd often close a week's worth of work in the first hours, then use the slack to optimize our Control-M job orchestration.

At GFT I learned to lead and to grow other engineers, and became the Java specialist people call for complex problems. Today I work in GFT's international unit, building payment systems for Mastercard.
